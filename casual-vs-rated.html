01 / Growth

Both formats grew; rated grew faster.

Across the first and last complete 12-month periods, casual play changed by +37.7% (478,675 → 659,197); rated play changed by +79.4% (350,152 → 628,041). Casual's share of combined games fell from 57.8% to 51.2%. These are counts, not a measure of unique people.

RatedCasual
September's casual spike is concentrated. Public account scissorsharpness recorded 63,504 casual games that month—55.6% of all casual September games, mostly Maia 1 at 1+0. Without this one account, September has 50,729 casual games versus 53,754 in August; last-year casual growth would be +24.4% rather than +37.7%. This is a sensitivity check, not a judgment about the account.

04 / Audit and access

Rebuild the comparison from the published metadata.

The casual export contains all 2,040,728 collected metadata rows in 12 compressed bot/year files. The rated export contains 1,425,567 rows. Both manifests include SHA-256 checksums, row counts, and field definitions. Run python3 scripts/build_casual_comparison.py at the repository root to recheck every shard and regenerate this page and the aggregate JSON.

Exports include game IDs, public handles where available, timestamps, results, speed, clock, opening labels, and recorded ratings. They do not include moves, PGNs, chat, or private identity mappings. The collection used date splitting below Lichess's observed response cap, then verified gap-free query-date coverage and in-scope last-move timestamps. A seven-day game-start lookback reduces—but cannot prove away—the possibility of an unusually long game that began earlier and finished inside the study window.
