01 / Your familiar sparring partners

Three Maias.
Many ways to practice.

Maia 1 is the busiest of the three: 58.6% of collected rated games were played against it. The higher-numbered bots attract higher-rated opponents, but all three draw a broad range of ratings.

This report looks at how accounts use Maia and whether their later results differ from their early ones. It does not measure move quality or prove that playing Maia causes improvement.

Later results are a little better.
But the story is mixed.

How we made the comparison

Same account. Same Maia. Same exact clock and speed category. At least 40 usable games, with at least 30 days between the early and later groups. One qualifying combination per account; each account gets equal weight.

“First” and “last” mean observed in this collection, not their first-ever or final Maia games. These accounts are a small, selected subset of the 93,020 observed accounts.

In the main group, mean recorded opponent rating rose 41.8 points, while Maia’s recorded rating rose 10.0. Accounts had White in 55.7% of early games and 55.3% of later games. These diagnostics do not fully control for bot changes or color.

People who return may differ from those who stop. Other chess practice, regression to the mean, changing bot strength, and ordinary luck can affect results. There is no comparison group and no move-quality analysis, so this is an association with repeated play, not a measured training effect.

05 / Behind the numbers

Curiosity, with receipts.

Every chart starts with public game metadata. You can download the records, inspect the definitions, and rebuild the report.

What we have

1,425,567 distinct game IDs from Maia 1, 5 and 9. Games with last recorded moves from 4 Oct 2023 00:00 UTC up to, but excluding, 4 Oct 2026 09:00 UTC. Twelve compressed CSV files contain dates, results, account handles, ratings, clocks and opening labels.

What we don’t

No moves, evaluations, move-by-move clocks, or casual-game history. No view of practice elsewhere. We cannot measure blunders, explain why someone returned, or attribute improvement to Maia.

What we checked

File hashes, row counts, unique game IDs and last-recorded-move time bounds. The collector’s date splitting was reported as complete; the public files cannot independently prove that the API omitted no games.
